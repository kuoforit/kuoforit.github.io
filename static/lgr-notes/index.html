01 / 術語速查

PDF
LG
Liquidity Gap，買賣力道失衡造成的價格缺口，日後會被回補。每個LG含High / Low / EQ三層。綁時間框，不可跨圖混用。
LGR
Liquidity Gap Reversal — 噴出造出LG後立刻收回LG之上/下，是swing point形成前的最後一個LG。單獨出現不代表任何事，只是「可能要開始整理」的提示。精確位置＝swing EQ往下（或往上）一根K棒（見第9節Flow Sequencing）。
LGC
Liquidity Gap Confirmation，Aggregation Model第3步：造出大LG的擴張K棒，作者內部原稱「fuck off candle」。代表確認要往下一級失衡流動噴。標記慣例是只用實體不用wick（見第8節）。
SSSL
Entry Trigger 1，四字母對應四步驟：Spike → Sweep → Swing → LGC。
DT
Directional Trigger，方向圖上用來確認「現在往哪走」的觸發，跟entry trigger分屬不同圖表。
EQ
兩種不同用法別搞混：① swing的EQ——拿整段swing高低點畫fib抓出的中點，第9節Flow Sequencing Phase 2判斷sweep有效性要用這個；② 單一K棒的EQ——這根K棒自己高低點的中點，第13節LE Candle判定用這個。兩者計算基礎不同，別互相套用。
LE Candle
Lose the Equilibrium — sweep後收盤穿越自己range EQ的K棒，作為SSSL的加強過濾器。
Sweep
掃過內部swing、或前一個swing裡某根K棒的高/低點。
Swing
方向轉折的三點結構（低-高-低 或 高-低-高）。判斷LG、LGR、EQ都要先確定「現在在哪個swing裡」——第7節專門講怎麼挑「相關的swing」。
Rebalance
market從inefficient（失衡/擴張）回頭整理、回補缺口的階段，小實體、低量。跟expansion（擴張/inefficient）交替出現，是Aggregation Model第2步。
OE
Origin of Expansion，動能剛啟動、造出第一個LG的那根K棒——市場之後要rebalance時，就是要回來補這個位置（第03節）。
HH / LL
Higher High / Lower Low，LE Candle判定sweep candle方向用的縮寫：做空要看HH，做多要看LL（第13節）。
Flows
市場目前的整體方向意圖（"flows are up/down"）。flows反轉＝完整走完一次Aggregation Model或5 Phase Cycle才算數，不是隨便一根K棒就能判定。
LG Level N
同一個swing裡由近到遠的編號系統：LGR＝LG Level 1，LGR之後第一個untested LG＝LG Level 2，依此類推。收盤穿越LG2不只是訊號，代表「情勢真的變了」——比LGR本身更重要（第8節）。
Reclaim
收盤穿越LG level後又跌破，之後再度收盤穿越同一個level——這個「二次收復」可以當成比第一次穿越更強的方向確認（第8節）。
Confluence
多個獨立層級（LG level、前一根LGC的EQ、swing內部結構）同時指向同一個價位，代表訊號強度更高。第9.5節「四層框架」的核心判準。
VC
Volatility Candle，帶大wick、顯示劇烈拉扯的K棒，常見於sweep附近，代表「fuel」正在被吃掉準備發動下一段。第12.2節Sweep DT的精修條件之一。

03 / 市場為什麼會動

影片逐字稿

整套系統最底層的世界觀。核心命題：市場移動是流動性的函數。交易所不是中立的撮合方，它們靠價差跟內部流動性賺錢，一旦偵測到動能（momentum），會啟動「price engine」把買賣價一路推到能吃到足夠訂單為止——這就是為什麼會有大wick跟跳空。

  • 市場只在兩種狀態間循環：efficient（平衡/整理）——小實體、缺口很快被填平；inefficient（失衡/擴張）——大實體、留下未回補的缺口。balanced → imbalanced → rebalance → balanced，無限循環。
  • 別在consolidation（平衡整理段）裡交易——那是低量、低流動性環境，方向不明。要在「consolidation的終結點」進場，也就是動能剛啟動的那一刻。
  • 動能開始的那根K棒＝expansion的origin（起源），之後市場要rebalance時，就是要回來補這個origin的位置。

06 / LG to LG 判方向

PDF ＋ 影片逐字稿
  • 先找出目前所在的swing，標出裡面所有LG
  • 優先看同一swing內未測試過的LG — 最敏感、機率最高
  • 同swing內測試完，往前一個swing找目標
  • LG綁時間框，判方向永遠只用同一張圖，不跨timeframe混用

逐K棒操作時，把這套規則刻進肌肉記憶的口訣是「close through it, go to the next one」：大擴張K棒造出的LG＝「objective level」，同一個swing裡沒被測試過的LG敏感度最高；swing內的untested LG都測試完了，就退回前一個swing找下一個目標——這跟下一節「怎麼挑相關的swing」是同一件事的兩種講法。距離大＝這段移動會更expansive，距離的長短本身就是一種資訊，值得放進判斷裡。

"We never front run levels."
反覆出現的警語：沒收盤穿越就不能提前當作已經發生。

07 / 怎麼挑「相關的swing」

影片逐字稿

怎麼決定該拿哪個swing來標LG。zoom out到最大常常會找到要花好幾週才會走到的LG，對短線交易沒有實際意義，所以要挑「相對於目前價格有意義」的swing。

核心規則：「圖上最重要的東西，是我們現在所在swing的前一個swing」——這個swing是你交易的依據，前一個swing是你參考的依據。一致性比選哪個swing更重要：只要每次都用同一套邏輯挑swing，規則就能穩定套用在每張圖上。

08 / LGR 與 LGC 的判定

影片逐字稿

LGC的標記慣例，以及LGC本身也是proportionate（成比例）的兩個判定細節。

  • 標LGC時只用實體（body）不用wick：「from the high to the close... I don't want the wicks, the wicks are unimportant to me, I want to play within the LGC」——目的是避免一根K棒裡混進好幾天的價格行為。
  • LGC本身也是proportionate：每根LGC自己內部也在做expansion跟rebalance，只是比例更小——這條線索其實已經預告了後面第10節的5 Phase Expansion Cycle。
  • 再次強調LGR永遠是swing形成前的最後一個LG，不是swing裡任何一個LG。

LG Level編號系統

Defining your Swing and your LG Levels

把LGR之後的層級精確命名：LGR＝LG Level 1，LGR之後第一個untested LG＝LG Level 2，再往後LG Level 3、4...。這條編號讓「我們現在在追蹤哪一層」變得可以明確指稱，不再只是含糊地說「下一個level」。

關鍵地位差異：「LGR signals a rebalance, but closing through LG Level 2 doesn't signal a rebalance — it signals that something has changed.」LGR只是提示可能要整理，LG Level 2才是真正判定「方向反轉是否成立」的關鍵level，是整套系統裡最重要的單一level。

Reclaim（來自Let's Talk LGCs）：收盤穿越LG後又跌破，之後再度收盤穿越同一個level——這個「二次收復」的訊號比第一次穿越更值得信任，可以當成SSSL/DT額外的加分確認。

09 / LGR System Flow Sequencing — 三階段SOP

影片逐字稿

方向模組的總結——把「該看到什麼才能行動」寫成明確的三階段SOP，其中Phase 2的EQ規則是目前所有教材裡唯一出現的地方，資訊密度是全書最高的一節。

9.1

三階段SOP

1
PHASE 1 — CLOSING THROUGH THE LG LEVEL
等前一個LG level被一根反方向的擴張K棒收盤穿越（例如市場原本在跌，卻收盤穿越一個往上的LG）——這只確認方向，但要等rebalance階段完成才算數。高時間框的訊號比較乾淨，每到一個level都要重新evaluate。
2
PHASE 2 — THE REBALANCE
必須同時看到：①噴進internal swing的LG或LGC ②這個噴進的位置要在整個swing的EQ之下（做多）或之上（做空）——EQ是拿整段swing的高低點畫fib抓出來的，不是單一根K棒的EQ ③掃到前一個swing point/LGC/K棒的高低點 ④掃出後未能延續，SSSL才算完成。
3
PHASE 3 — THE EXPANSION
確認擴張展開後，切到進場時間框逐K棒（candle by candle）操作，每個higher low/lower high都是機會，直到打到下一級flows再重新evaluate。
Points to Note（原片檢核清單）：噴進internal level的幅度越大，反轉機率越高；找不到LG level就往上一個時間框找；LG/LGC的大小本身是資訊——越大的擴張，之後需要的rebalance也越久；LGR level的精確定義＝swing EQ往下（或往上）一根，這就是origin of expansion的位置；掃到之後不該逗留，該看到brief aggregation後迅速展開。

9.3Candle by Candle算的起點是哪裡

Why Lower Highs and Higher Lows are so Damn Important

Phase 3寫「切到進場時間框逐K棒操作」，容易誤解成從整個swing開始算。真正的起點更精確：

TraderxB · 影片原話
"I'm candle by candle from here, on the lower high. I'm not candle by candle on the swing."

計時起點是確認出higher low/lower high的那根K棒，不是swing剛形成的地方。信心水位他自己估：「85 to 90%... if you can glean 75 to 80% of the direction then you're good to go」——不用追求100%確定才動作。

9.4別觀察過頭

Ramblings on Logic, LG to LG, and Candle by Candle

最常見的錯誤：看到反方向的LGR就整個進入observation，一路等到出現同方向LGC才肯回來——中間這段其實已經漏掉真正的進場點。

TraderxB · 影片原話
"LGRs are not of any importance other than to tell you rebalance is coming... we don't observe until we get this candle [失敗延續的那根K棒]. What you've done [提早進observation] is you've missed the opportunity within this candle."

9.5LGC四層框架

Bringing it All Together

整套系統可以收斂成一句話：永遠只在四個層級之一交易——① LGC的High　② LGC的EQ　③ LGC的Low　④ LG level本身。這四層之外的價格波動基本上不值得關注。

Confluence（多層重合）：當這四層裡有多個同時指向同一個價位——例如「LG level」剛好跟「前一根LGC的EQ」疊在一起——訊號強度會顯著提高。這是判斷「這個進場點夠不夠好」最實用的單一判準。

12 / 進場後的管理與精修

影片逐字稿

風控與Directional Trigger精修合併成一節，因為兩者本質上都是「SSSL進場之後該怎麼做」——一個管風險，一個管挑DT跟精修進場點。

12.1

R倍數移動停損

來自Managing Risk Parameters on a Live SSSL entry。進場後不是死守原始停損到底，而是隨著價格每收盤穿越一個R倍數，就把停損移到「造成那次穿越的那個swing」——不是固定百分比的移動停損，是用結構本身當停損參照。

TraderxB · 影片原話
"If I can guarantee 3R per trade, then my strike rate all it has to be is above 35%, and I'm making money."
他自己的取捨：他是「hit and quit」交易者，目標定死3R，不分批停利，靠移動停損鎖住獲利。如果系統勝率已經驗證到60~65%，其實不需要這套移動停損；勝率還在累積數據階段，就該積極移動停損降低風險。
12.2

三種DT類型

來自Defining your DTs與Homework - The Sweep DT。他把自己實際在用的DT收斂成三種，難度跟勝率排序如下：

Flow Continuation LGC
最容易辨認：LGC單純延續現有flows方向，收盤穿越前一個LG level。這是三者中最安全、最好上手的。
LGC Reversal
LGC不只是延續flows，而是收盤穿越前一根LGC本身（不只是穿越LG level）。他自估這是三者中最賺錢的一種，但判讀難度也最高。
Sweep DT（4R Sweep）
最複雜、唯一會逆著當下flows走的DT類型——下跌趨勢中的lower-high sweep才是「bread and butter」，higher-high sweep機率較低。精修規則：沒有前置LGR、或沒看到VC（Volatility Candle，大wick顯示劇烈拉扯）的話，不輕易把sweep當DT。
12.3

Retest精修進場

來自Refining DTs and Entries using the same DT。標準SSSL是在LGC收盤當下市價進場；精修版是等LGC出現後，回測（retest）那根LGC的high/EQ/low才進場，用更好的價格換更佳風報比。

實際效益：「Half your entry-to-stop distance means double position size for same risk」——進場點離停損更近，同樣風險金額下部位可以放大一倍。代價是不一定每次都會拿到retest，錯過了就照原始SSSL規則市價進場，不強求。
12.4

多時間框精修：Weekly → Daily → 4H

來自Refining your Levels for Higher Accuracy。具體工作流程：每週日花10分鐘看Weekly抓level，每天看Daily抓level，再把這些更高時間框的level「轉寫」到4H圖上——4H上剛好跟Weekly/Daily的level重合的位置，就是confluence最強的地方，通常會落在4H LGC的EQ附近。

額外情報：他提到自己也有跑一套「現貨週期交易」，用Daily判方向、4R進場，2021整年大概只出現20次訊號，平均持倉約2週9天（最短3天、最長超過1個月）——時間框越高，訊號越少，但風報比也跟著放大。
一個變體：同樣是Daily→4H的精修技巧，但LGC三層改用Open / EQ / Close來描述，不是其他章節慣用的High/EQ/Low。跟LG level重合的那一層通常是EQ最敏感，跟第9.5節的四層框架邏輯一致，只是這裡換了一種切法描述同一組層級。

18 / 案例研究 — BTC盤面實況

影片逐字稿

18.1

LG to LG連續推進＋SSSL全流程

BTC即時盤面分析，單一session走完一次完整的「方向判斷→進場判斷」流程。

開場先示範一個不用先畫線就能大致判斷市場狀態的直覺：小實體帶長wick＝efficient（整理），大實體＝inefficient（擴張）。接著標出一段從高點噴出的大LGR，收盤穿越後市場一路efficient噴到高點——因為高點之上沒有untested LG，訂單都在那，價格自然直奔那裡。到了高點出現一根吃盡雙邊range的K棒，隨後大幅噴出穿越前一根LGC，形成新的LGR，這根K棒被明講「必須是DT，沒有模糊空間」，示範什麼叫「毫無疑義的訊號」。

接著具體示範「LG大小本身是資訊」的用法：LGC越大，代表它產生的LG level之後需要的aggregation（整理）時間也越長——大level＝訂單量大＝需要更久時間消化；並用「books are thin」解釋一個level被測試過一次之後敏感度會下降的原因：原本堆在那裡的單邊訂單被掃過之後就被清空了，之後即使價格回來，也不會有一樣多的量在等。全片最具體的段落，是一根K棒同時滿足「跌破前高後failure to continue」「穿越LG level」「inside bar後收盤形成LGC」「同時也是LGR」多重confluence，於是這根K棒的整個高低範圍都被視為可交易區間，在裡面找SSSL進場，目標設在LGC high。

TraderxB · 影片原話
"The bigger the LGC, the larger the move that precedes it and also the larger the aggregation."
LG/LGC大小本身帶資訊：越大的擴張，之後需要的整理期也越長。

23.1

停損：結構性移動停損

來自第12.1節。進場後不是死守原始停損，而是隨著價格每收盤穿越一個R倍數，就把停損移到「造成那次穿越的那個swing」——用結構本身當停損參照，不是固定百分比。

取捨：系統勝率已驗證到60~65%時，其實不需要這套移動停損；勝率還在累積數據階段，就該積極移動停損降低風險。另一種軟停損做法（第20.2節現貨策略）：不掛在交易所訂單簿裡，而是在關鍵level設alert，觸發後才手動評估。
23.2

部位大小：順勢全額、逆勢減半

來自第19.1節90天回測案例。逆勢單位（sweep DT等反著當下flows走的訊號）建議減半風險；順勢單位（flows continuation LGC，第12.2節三種DT類型裡最安全的一種）才給全額風險。

23.3

停利：固定倍數 vs. 鎖外層range

兩種做法並存，取決於策略：SSSL標準做法（第11節）是固定倍數2:1/3:1（6:1較激進，作者不建議）；現貨long-only策略（第20.2節）則只鎖external range的LG，不看固定R倍數，實際RR經驗值落在2.75~3.25R之間，因為「市場習慣走三段式節奏」。

教材沒有涵蓋、需要自己另外補的：單筆交易該押多少%本金、同時開幾筆倉位算安全、連續虧損後該怎麼調整——這些帳戶層級的資金管理規則，翻遍全部教材跟案例都沒有找到明確數字。永續合約特有的資金費率（funding rate）怎麼處理、槓桿倍數怎麼選、市價單滑價怎麼應對，教材也完全沒有觸及。這些是交易所執行面的知識，需要自己另外建立規則，不是這套系統教的範圍。

01 / Terminology Quick Reference

PDF
LG
Liquidity Gap — a price gap created by an imbalance of buying/selling force, which will eventually get filled back in. Every LG has three layers: High / Low / EQ. Tied to its timeframe — never mix across charts.
LGR
Liquidity Gap Reversal — a spike that creates an LG and is immediately reclaimed back above/below it; it's the last LG before a swing point forms. On its own it means nothing — it's only a hint that "a rebalance might be starting." Its precise location = one candle below (or above) the swing EQ (see Section 9, Flow Sequencing).
LGC
Liquidity Gap Confirmation, step 3 of the Aggregation Model: the expansion candle that creates a large LG, internally nicknamed the "fuck off candle" by the author. It confirms the spike toward the next level of imbalanced flow. Convention is to mark it by body only, not wick (see Section 8).
SSSL
Entry Trigger 1 — four letters for four steps: Spike → Sweep → Swing → LGC.
DT
Directional Trigger — the trigger on the direction chart used to confirm "which way we're going now," on a separate chart from the entry trigger.
EQ
Two different uses — don't mix them up: ① The swing's EQ — the midpoint from a fib drawn across the whole swing's high/low, used in Section 9's Flow Sequencing Phase 2 to judge whether a sweep is valid; ② A single candle's EQ — the midpoint of that candle's own high/low, used by the Section 13 LE Candle rule. The two are computed on different bases — don't apply one where the other belongs.
LE Candle
Lose the Equilibrium — a candle that closes through its own range's EQ after a sweep, used as a stronger filter on SSSL.
Sweep
Sweeping past an internal swing, or the high/low of some candle in the previous swing.
Swing
The three-point structure of a directional turn (low-high-low, or high-low-high). Judging LG, LGR, and EQ all require first pinning down "which swing are we in right now" — Section 7 is devoted to how to pick the "relevant swing."
Rebalance
The phase where the market pulls back from inefficient (imbalanced/expansion) and fills its gap in — small bodies, low volume. Alternates with expansion (imbalanced), and is step 2 of the Aggregation Model.
OE
Origin of Expansion — the candle where momentum first kicks off and creates the first LG; when the market later rebalances, it's coming back to fill in this spot (Section 3).
HH / LL
Higher High / Lower Low — abbreviations used by the LE Candle rule to judge the sweep candle's direction: for shorts look at HH, for longs look at LL (Section 13).
Flows
The market's current overall directional intent ("flows are up/down"). A flow reversal only counts once a full Aggregation Model or 5 Phase Cycle has completed — not something a single candle can decide.
LG Level N
A near-to-far numbering system within the same swing: LGR = LG Level 1, the first untested LG after the LGR = LG Level 2, and so on. Closing through LG2 isn't just a signal — it means "things have genuinely changed" — more important than the LGR itself (Section 8).
Reclaim
Closing through an LG level, then dropping back below it, then closing through that same level again — this "second reclaim" can be treated as a stronger directional confirmation than the first close-through (Section 8).
Confluence
Several independent levels (an LG level, the EQ of the previous LGC, internal swing structure) all pointing at the same price at once, meaning the signal is stronger. The core criterion of Section 9.5's "four-layer framework."
VC
Volatility Candle — a candle with a large wick showing violent back-and-forth, commonly seen near a sweep, meaning "fuel" is being consumed to set up the next leg. One of the refinement conditions for the Sweep DT in Section 12.2.

03 / Why the Market Moves

Video transcript

The most fundamental worldview underlying the whole system. The core thesis: market movement is a function of liquidity. Exchanges are not neutral matchmakers — they profit off spread and internal liquidity, and once they detect momentum, they run a "price engine" that pushes bid/ask along until it's eaten enough orders — this is why you get big wicks and gaps.

  • The market only cycles between two states: efficient (balanced/consolidating) — small bodies, gaps filled quickly; inefficient (imbalanced/expanding) — large bodies, gaps left unfilled. balanced → imbalanced → rebalance → balanced, on an endless loop.
  • Don't trade inside consolidation (a balanced, consolidating stretch) — that's a low-volume, low-liquidity environment with no clear direction. Enter at "the end of consolidation" instead — the exact moment momentum kicks off.
  • The candle where momentum begins = the origin of the expansion; whenever the market later rebalances, it's coming back to fill in this origin's position.

06 / Reading Direction with LG to LG

PDF + video transcript
  • First find the swing you're currently in, and mark every LG inside it
  • Prioritize LGs untested within the same swing — most sensitive, highest probability
  • Once everything in the current swing is tested, look for the next target in the previous swing
  • LG is tied to its timeframe — reading direction always uses the same single chart, never mixed across timeframes

For candle-by-candle work, the mnemonic that drills this rule into muscle memory is "close through it, go to the next one": the LG created by a big expansion candle is an "objective level," and within a given swing, the untested LG is the most sensitive; once every untested LG in a swing has been tested, drop back to the previous swing for the next target — this is the same idea as the next section's "picking the relevant swing," just phrased two ways. A bigger distance means a more expansive move — the size of the distance is itself information worth factoring into your read.

07 / How to Pick the "Relevant Swing"

How to decide which swing to use for marking LG. Zooming all the way out often turns up an LG that will take weeks to reach, which has no practical use for short-term trading — so you need to pick the swing that's "meaningful relative to the current price."

08 / Judging LGR and LGC

Video transcript

Two judgment details: the marking convention for LGC, and the fact that LGC is itself proportionate.

  • When marking an LGC, use only the body, not the wick: "from the high to the close... I don't want the wicks, the wicks are unimportant to me, I want to play within the LGC" — the point is to avoid mixing several days' worth of price action into a single candle.
  • LGC itself is proportionate too: every LGC is internally running its own expansion and rebalance, just at a smaller scale — this detail already foreshadows Section 10's 5 Phase Expansion Cycle.
  • A reminder that LGR is always the last LG before a swing forms, not just any LG within a swing.

The LG Level Numbering System

Defining your Swing and your LG Levels

Names the layers after the LGR precisely: LGR = LG Level 1, the first untested LG after the LGR = LG Level 2, then LG Level 3, 4... This numbering lets you refer to "which layer we're tracking now" explicitly, instead of vaguely saying "the next level."

A key difference in standing: "LGR signals a rebalance, but closing through LG Level 2 doesn't signal a rebalance — it signals that something has changed." LGR only hints that a rebalance might be coming; LG Level 2 is what actually determines whether a directional reversal is confirmed — the single most important level in the whole system.

Reclaim (from Let's Talk LGCs): closing through an LG, dropping back below it, then closing through that same level again — this "second reclaim" signal is more trustworthy than the first close-through, and can serve as an extra confirming point for SSSL/DT.

23 / Risk Management, Consolidated

Synthesis

This section isn't a new rule — it's the risk-management details originally scattered across Sections 12.1, 19.1, and 20.2, pulled together so they can be read in one pass. This is a cross-section synthesis; no single video maps word-for-word onto how this section is organized.

23.1

Stop-Loss: Structural Trailing Stops

From Section 12.1. After entry, don't just white-knuckle the original stop — instead, every time price closes through an R-multiple, move the stop to "the swing that caused that close-through" — not a fixed-percentage trailing stop, but one that references the structure itself.

The trade-off: once a system's win rate has been validated at 60-65%, this trailing-stop scheme isn't really necessary; while the win rate is still being proven out with accumulating data, you should trail the stop aggressively to cut risk. An alternative soft-stop approach (Section 20.2's spot strategy): rather than sitting in the exchange's order book, it's an alert set at the key level, manually evaluated once triggered.
23.2

Position Size: Full Risk With the Trend, Half Risk Against It

From Section 19.1's 90-day backtest case. Counter-trend positions (signals going against the current flows, like a sweep DT) should get half risk; with-trend positions (a flow-continuation LGC, the safest of the three DT types in Section 12.2) get full risk.

23.3

Take-Profit: A Fixed Multiple vs. Locking In the Outer Range

Two approaches coexist depending on the strategy: the standard SSSL approach (Section 11) is a fixed 2:1/3:1 multiple (6:1 is more aggressive and not recommended by the author); the spot long-only strategy (Section 20.2) only locks in the external range's LG rather than watching a fixed R-multiple, though in practice the realized RR tends to land around 2.75-3.25R, because "the market tends to move in threes."

What the material doesn't cover, and you need to build yourself: how much of an account to risk per trade, how many concurrent positions is safe, how to adjust after a losing streak — none of this account-level money-management guidance appears anywhere in the material or the case studies, no matter how thoroughly you search. Perpetual-futures-specific concerns — how to handle the funding rate, how to choose leverage, how to deal with market-order slippage — are also completely untouched by the material. These are exchange-execution-level considerations you'll need to build your own rules for; they're outside the scope of what this system teaches.

24 / Entry Decision Checklist

Synthesis

Direction judgment → DT trigger → SSSL entry → filter → stop/take-profit → position size, strung together into an actionable checklist. This is a cross-section synthesis; no single video maps word-for-word onto this checklist, and real screen judgment still requires the intuition built up through practice (see Section 10 and 18.3's "even the author admits it's subjective").

1
Confirm Direction
Use LG to LG (Section 6) to mark the swing you're currently in and every LG inside it, prioritizing any untested LG within the same swing. Confirm you've picked the right "relevant swing" (Section 7).
2
Confirm the DT Trigger
Which of the three DT types (Section 12.2) is this: Flow Continuation LGC (safest), LGC Reversal (most profitable but hardest to read), or Sweep DT (most complex, counter-trend)? Is there confluence (Section 9.5's four-tier framework) pointing at the same price at once?
3
The SSSL Four Steps
Has Spike → Sweep → Swing → LGC (Section 11) appeared in sequence? LE Candle filter (Section 13): is the sweep candle's high/low more extreme than the previous one, and has the close crossed its own range's EQ?
4
Refinement (Optional)
Is it worth waiting for a retest of the LGC for a better risk/reward (Section 12.3)? Does a higher-timeframe level show confluence here (Section 12.4's multi-timeframe refinement)?
5
Stop-Loss, Take-Profit, and Position Size
Set the stop a few ticks above/below the swing, targeting 2:1/3:1 (Section 11). Is this a with-trend or counter-trend position? Has the position risk been adjusted per the rule in Section 23.2?
6
Post-Trade Check
Was this trade forced? "If you're patient the setups will come to you" (Section 9) — until you see a clear signal, the answer is always to wait, never to go looking for a reason to enter.
A reminder: this checklist helps you confirm you've checked everything you should — it doesn't do the subjective judgment for you. Where to mark the origin of expansion, whether the market is "really aggregating" right now — those still depend on the intuition built up practicing the cases in Sections 18-21. There's no shortcut.
